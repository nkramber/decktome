## The judge's reasons

- 1. lifegain Commander, any card: graded bad, judged typical. This is the standard community lifegain build for Karlov 
cleric/lifegain payoffs like Heliod, Vito, Well of Lost Dreams and Ocelot Pride are on-plan, but the deck is padded with weak filler (Hot Dog Cart, Colossal Plow, Bounty Board, Nuka-Cola Vending Machine, Oasis Gardener) and only two real fast lands amid 27 basics. Ramp is slow rocks rather than Sol Ring/Talisman-tier acceleration, and removal is thin beyond Solitude, Murderous Rider and Kaya's Wrath, with no tutors or fast combo finish. Cohesive and playable, clearly above precon, but no sharp edge.
- 2. aristocrats Commander, owned first: graded good, judged typical. This is a solid but unfocused midrange Orzhov build \u2014 decent removal suite (Infernal Grasp, Generous Gift, Austere Command, Vanquish the Horde), fine card draw, and reasonable protection for the commander, but the mana base is largely basics with pain lands and no real duals/fetches, and the ramp stops at cheap rocks. The synergy is scattered: some -1/-1 counter pieces (Blowfly Infestation, Contagion Clasp, Rat King), some equipment/Puresteel, some Ring-tempts, none of it deeply committed to Denethor's discard/sacrifice plan. It looks and plays like the average community build of this commander \u2014 functional, no sharp edge.
- 3. artifacts Commander, bracket 4: graded bad, judged typical. It has the Urza skeleton \u2014 cost reducers, Metalworker, Mystic Forge, Mox Opal, Workshops, Emry \u2014 but no actual wincon or combo package (no Winter Orb/Thopter-Sword/Isochron lock, no Gaea's Cradle-tier ramp, almost no fast mana beyond Opal). The 20 basic Islands plus a pile of filler artifact creatures (Rust Elemental, Panther Robot, Darksteel Juggernaut, Cargo Ship) and one-for-one removal like Water Whip and Zuko's Exile put it well below tuned lists. Solid synergy and a real curve, but it's the average community Urza build rather than a competitive one.
- 4. dinosaur tribal, bracket 2: graded bad, judged typical. The mana base is genuinely strong for a three-color tribal deck — full duals, Cavern of Souls, Three Tree City, and eight ramp pieces — and the plan is coherent Gishath dinosaur beats with hexproof/haste protection. But the creature suite is padded with weak filler (Raptor Companion, Colossal Dreadmaw, Nest Robber, Dinosaur Egg, Drowsing Tyrannodon) and the curve is very top-heavy, so it can stall out before Gishath lands. Interaction is thin beyond three sweepers and Trumpeting Carnosaur, which is about where the average community Gishath build sits.
- 5. blink Commander, owned first: graded good, judged typical. This is a solid mono-white Gilraen build with the expected shell — Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Swords to Plowshares/Get Lost, equipment protection, blink and aura payoffs — but it's diffuse rather than focused, mixing flicker-value creatures, an aura/equipment subtheme, and three wraths that fight its own creature-based plan. The mana base is functional but land-light on utility (27 basics plus a few taplands) and the card quality is community-average, with several filler picks (PuPu UFO, Mirror of Galadriel, Stone of Erech, Crown of Gondor) instead of white's premium staples. It plays fine and wins games at a normal table, but there's no sharp engine or fast clock.
- 6. Modern tempo, tournament: graded good, judged typical. The mana base and interaction suite (Counterspell, Bolt, Pierce, Into the Flood Maw) are Modern-legitimate, and Ledger Shredder plus cantrips is a real shell, but 4 Temporal Mastery is a serious liability \value payless-cost only with a stacked top and otherwise a 7-mana blank, and 4 Faerie Mastermind is a weak maindeck body. Real izzet tempo lists want Ragavan, Murktide/Psychic Frog, Mishra's Bauble and fetch-supported deck manipulation rather than 26 basically untuned lands and no fetches to enable miracle or delirium. It plays like an unrefined but coherent version of the archetype.
- 7. Modern burn, casual: graded bad, judged bad. This is mono-red burn with a bloated 24-land mana base and a pile of unplayable four-drop creatures (Akki Lavarunner, Keral Keep Disciples, Fuming Effigy) where Modern burn wants Goblin Guide, Monastery Swiftspear, and Eidolon of the Great Revel plus efficient reach like Lava Spike, Boros Charm, and Skewer the Critics. Risk Factor/Browbeat hand the opponent the choice and Roiling Vortex is a sideboard hate card, not a maindeck clock, so the deck's damage-per-mana is far worse than a precon-level aggro build. The curve is top-heavy, there is no burn density, and the synergy between prison effects and bad creatures simply doesn't exist.
- 8. Modern lifegain, FNM: graded bad, judged bad. This is a lifegain pile with Arcane Signet (not Modern-legal in spirit of playability — a 2-mana rock that does nothing in a 2-color deck), a 24-land mana base that only has 4 duals for a deck full of double-costed four- and five-drops, and multiple weak payoffs like Bloodbond Vampire and Ajani's Pridemate that do nothing against Modern's real threats. Solitude and Sheoldred are strong cards stranded in a shell that can't support them, and Bloodthirsty Conqueror/Enduring Tenacity are do-nothing five-drops with no fast lifegain engine. The curve is top-heavy, the interaction is thin, and the synergy pieces are below precon quality for the format.
- 9. Standard midrange, FNM: graded good, judged typical. The core is real Standard material — Bitter Triumph, Assassin's Trophy, Vein Ripper and Vaultborn Tyrant behind Undying Malice and Snakeskin Veil — and the two-color mana base with Blooming Marsh, Overgrown Tomb and Underground Mortuary is clean. But the build is clunky where the tuned versions aren't: 24 lands and only two Llanowar Elves to support a stack of five- and six-drops, and four Phyrexian Arena plus Annex piling on life loss in a deck already paying for removal. It plays like the average midrange-into-fatties list rather than the sharpened one that wins a Challenge.
- 10. Standard aggro, tournament: graded bad, judged bad. It runs 4 Boros Charm, which isn't Standard-legal, and the mana base is 24 basics with none of the format's Boros duals (Elegant Parlor, Sunbaked Canyon, Battlefield Forge, Restless Bivouac), which is a real cost for a two-color deck wanting double-colored 4-drops. The one-drop slot is a pile of scattered 2-ofs instead of playsets, so the aggro curve is inconsistent, and the top end of Warleader's Call plus Redcap Gutter-Dweller clashes with a deck that has no way to actually get on the board early. Interaction is just Harsh Annotation, and the whole thing doesn't cohere around a plan.
- 11. Commander with a locked card: graded bad, judged typical. It's a coherent aristocrats build with strong engine pieces (Yawgmoth, Grave Pact, Priest of Forgotten Gods, free sac outlets, Bastion/Cutthroat drains), but it barely uses Karlov's lifegain-counter plan and leans on generic sac-value cards instead. The mana base is 29 basics plus a handful of duals with essentially no ramp beyond Sol Ring and the altars, and interaction is limited to a few wraths and Attrition. Playable and on-plan, but far from tuned \u2014 the average community Karlov/aristocrats list.
- 12. Commander on a budget: graded bad, judged typical. This is the standard mono-white Adeline token build: plenty of on-theme token producers and anthems, but the mana base is 30 basic Plains with almost no ramp (no Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Smothering Tithe, or white staples like Land Tax/Mana Crypt), so it plays out slowly. Interaction is thin and generic /Skyclave Apparition, Generous Gift, Stroke of Midnight/ with a heavy top-end of five-plus drops, and several filler artifacts (Coin of Mastery, Goldvein Pick, Collector's Vault, Charisma Bobblehead) dilute the plan. Synergy is coherent and the payoffs are real, but nothing here is sharp or fast by format standards.
- 13. owned first, and the commander is not owned: graded bad, judged typical. It's an on-theme lifegain/Karlov build with solid removal (Swords, Push, Get Lost, Infernal Grasp) and fine ramp, but the mana base is 36 basics with essentially no duals, filter lands, or Command Tower, which is well below even average for a two-color deck. Payoffs are diffuse — lots of small lifegain creatures and equipment that don't converge on a fast Karlov kill, and there's no tutor package or strong wincon beyond combat damage. Reasonable and coherent, but no sharp edge.
- 14. the user delegates the commander: graded bad, judged typical. This is a solid, coherent Gruul dragon-tribal build: cost reducers, Dragon Tempest/Scourge of Valkas payoffs, card draw engines like Elemental Bond and Garruk's Uprising, and a decent mana base with Taiga, Stomping Ground and Cavern of Souls. But ramp is thin and slow (Orb-of-Dragonkind cycle, Dragon's Hoard) for a deck full of six- and seven-drops, and interaction is mostly narrow red removal plus a couple of protection spells rather than the format's best answers. It plays like the average well-loved dragon list \u2014 fun and on-plan, but with no sharp edge or speed.
- 15. delegated commander, owned first: graded bad, judged typical. This is a lifegain/counters W/B midrange list built roughly as the community builds such a commander: solid staple removal (Swords, Get Lost, Infernal Grasp, Generous Gift) and decent card draw, but the payoff creatures (Angel of Vitality, Compassionate Healer, Kor Firewalker, Shattered Angel) are low-impact and the deck lacks a real win condition or fast mana beyond Sol Ring. The mana base is 24 basics plus a handful of tapless utility lands — fine for casual but pain lands and tribal lands in a two-color deck are unnecessary filler, and the curve leans heavy on 4-5 drops. Reasonable and internally coherent, with no sharp edge or tuned engine.
- 16. a tight budget, owned first: graded bad, judged typical. It's a coherent Denethor aristocrats build with a solid drain package (Blood Artist, Zulaport, Bastion of Remembrance, Elas il-Kor) and a genuinely good removal suite, but the payoffs outnumber the fodder — there's very little token generation to feed the sacrifice engines, and board wipes like Fumigate/Austere Command fight the go-wide plan. The mana is two-color safe but unambitious: 29 basics with no shocks, duals, or filter lands, and Sol Ring plus a few three-mana rocks is the whole ramp. Reasonable community-standard construction with no sharp edge.
- 17. upgrade a precon, owned first: graded good, judged typical. It's a coherent mono-red goblin swarm build with a light Zada cantrip package (Crimson Wisps, Expedite, Ancestral Anger, Haze of Rage, Fists of Flame) plus solid payoffs like Impact Tremors, Goblin Bombardment and Skullclamp, but the two plans dilute each other — the goblin tribal shell rarely produces enough targeted pump for a real Zada turn. Mana is functional yet unambitious: 25 Mountains, Sol Ring, Ruby Medallion and a few rituals, with none of the format's better accelerants, and interaction is limited to Chaos Warp and Vandalblast. Sound and on-theme, roughly how the community averages this commander, without the tight cantrip density or tutors a sharp list would run.
- 18. upgrade a precon, any card: graded good, judged baseline. This is essentially the TMNT precon shell with a handful of sensible additions (Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Esper Sentinel, Mystic Remora, Nature's Lore/Farseek, Garruk's Uprising, a decent removal package), but the bulk is still bulk-rate Universes Beyond flavor cards with no unified engine beyond generic counters/creature beats. The mana is the real problem: five colors supported almost entirely by tapped duals, Thriving lands, cycling fetches, and 13 basics, with only three rocks and two of them not real ramp, so the curve regularly stumbles. Playable and on-color, but slow and generic — precon strength with light polish.
- 19. the Hobbit family, two colours: graded bad, judged bad. Aside from The One Ring, Orcish Bowmasters and a couple of accelerants, this is a pile of Middle-earth limited commons (Mirkwood Elk, Nimrodel Watcher, Elven Raft-Steerer) with no unified engine beyond a vague elves/spider theme that Thranduil doesn't actually reward. The mana is the fatal flaw: a three-color deck running 36 basics with zero duals, fetches, or fixing beyond Arcane Signet, Wayfarer's Bauble and Wood Elves will stumble on colors constantly, and Mox Amber is nearly blank with so few legends. Interaction is present but low-grade (Bilbo's Deadly Slice, Quarrel, Gnashing of Teeth) rather than the format's efficient answers, putting it below precon strength.
- 20. the Hobbit family, a delegated commander: graded bad, judged baseline. This is essentially a mono-red LOTR-themed pile: 31 Mountains plus a handful of utility lands, only Arcane Signet, Mox Amber and Wayfarer's Bauble for ramp, and almost no interaction beyond Goblin Cratermaker and a couple of burn spells. The card pool is dominated by limited-caliber creatures (Long-Bodied Grey Dog, Troop of Ponies, Snowslope Hunter, Oliphaunt) with no coherent engine tying the equipment subtheme to the Dwarf/Dragon bodies. The One Ring and Cavern-Hoard Dragon are the only cards near format-best, so it plays out at roughly precon power and speed.
- 21. the Hobbit family, mana from outside: graded bad, judged typical. This is a straightforward LOTR-themed Rakdos treasure/equipment build around Smaug: the fetch-heavy mana base plus Ancient Tomb, The One Ring, Orcish Bowmasters and Mox Amber are strong pieces, but most of the deck is on-theme set filler (Well-Worn Spatula, Oliphaunt, Desert Were-Worm, Great Ugly-Looking Goblin) rather than format staples. Interaction is thin and mostly conditional removal, and the curve skews high with several six-plus drops that a treasure ramp package only sometimes supports. It plays like a well-assembled casual list \u no better than how the average table builds this commander.
- 22. a set family and a card from outside it: graded bad, judged bad. 36 basic lands split evenly across three colors with essentially no dual lands or fixing beyond Arcane Signet/Delighted Halfling is a broken mana base for a Sultai deck, so the many two- and three-color spells will strand. The card pool is largely low-impact Tales of Middle-earth commons (Nasty Little Rabbit, Gigantic Big Bear, Mirkwood Elk) with almost no ramp and thin interaction, and the elf-tribal pieces like Elvish Archdruid conflict with the mostly non-Elf creature base. The One Ring, Sol Ring, and Orcish Bowmasters can't carry a shell this unfocused.
- 23. two set families at once: graded good, judged typical. This is a solid mono-white kindred/tokens build with real staples (Sol Ring, Swords to Plowshares, Skullclamp, Skyclave Apparition, Dusk // Dawn) and a coherent Andúril/Dwarf-token plan around Three Tree City, but it's diluted by filler like Bumbleflower's Sharepot, Heirloom Epic, Tangle Tumbler and Psychosis Crawler. Ramp is mostly slow rocks and the interaction package is thin for the power level, so it plays like a well-assembled but unsharpened casual list. Mana base is fine though basic-heavy with a few colorless utility lands that don't cost much in mono-white.
- 24. a 60-card deck from one set: graded bad, judged baseline. The creature core (Emberheart Challenger, Frilled Sparkshooter, Teapot Slinger) points at a coherent mono-red valiant/prowess plan, but the deck plays none of the format's actual red staples \u2014 no Lightning Bolt, no Monastery Swiftspear or Ragavan, no Slickshot Show-Off \u2014 and leans on soft filler like Agate Assault and Sazacap's Brew. Arcane Signet is dead weight in a one- and two-drop aggro shell, and 24 lands is three or four too many for that curve, so it floods badly. It casts its spells and attacks, which puts it at precon strength but nowhere near a competitive Modern list.

Verdict: FAIL.
