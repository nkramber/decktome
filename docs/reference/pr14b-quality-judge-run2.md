## The judge's reasons

- 1. lifegain Commander, any card: graded bad, judged typical. This is a coherent lifegain-payoff Karlov build with the right marquee pieces (Heliod, Vito, Serra Ascendant, Well of Lost Dreams, Solitude, Amalia, Ocelot Pride), so the synergy plan is real. But the mana base is 27 basics with only a handful of duals and no fast mana beyond weak trinket rocks like Hot Dog Cart, Colossal Plow and Orazca Relic, which makes the curve slow and clunky. Interaction is thin and mostly narrow protection effects rather than the format's efficient answers, putting it right at the community-average build.
- 2. aristocrats Commander, owned first: graded bad, judged typical. This is a solid but unfocused midrange Orzhov build: good removal density and card draw, plus Sol Ring, Skullclamp and Orcish Bowmasters, but the mana base is 27 basics with almost no duals, fetches or fast mana. The theme wanders between Ring-tempts, equipment/voltron protection, sacrifice and -1/-1 counters without committing to any of them, and Denethor's discard/sac ability is barely supported. It plays like a well-appointed average build of the commander rather than a tuned one.
- 3. artifacts Commander, bracket 4: graded bad, judged typical. The shell is on-plan mono-blue artifacts with cost reducers, Emry/Sai/Riddlesmith and a few real staples (Mox Opal, Mystic Forge, Metalworker, Academy Ruins, Mishra's Workshop), so it functions. But it's badly diluted: no Force of Will/Mana Drain/Cyclonic Rift tier interaction, a pile of one-off removal auras and weak beaters (Rust Elemental, Cargo Ship, Darksteel Juggernaut, Somber Hoverguard), and 20 basic Islands with almost no fast mana or tutors. No combo finish means it's a solid casual Urza build rather than a tuned one.
- 4. dinosaur tribal, bracket 2: graded bad, judged good. The mana base is genuinely strong for a three-color tribal deck — real duals, Cavern of Souls, Path of Ancestry, plus a decent ramp package to get Gishath online — and the synergy pieces (Otepec Huntmaster, Kinjalli's Caller, Regisaur Alpha, Marauding Raptor, Temple Altisaur, Garruk's Uprising) all point at the commander's plan. It's held back by filler beaters like Colossal Dreadmaw, Raptor Companion and Dinosaur Egg, thin targeted interaction, and no real haste/protection redundancy or tutors, so it plays a turn or two behind the sharpest builds. Solid, on-plan list that would grind out a winning league record without threatening a top cut.
- 5. blink Commander, owned first: graded good, judged typical. This is a competent mono-white Gilraen build: solid ramp package, a deep white removal suite, and equipment/protection pieces that loosely support the commander's counter-and-flicker plan. But the mana base is 27 basics with only utility trimmings, and the card pool leans on filler (Mirror of Galadriel, Stone of Erech, PuPu UFO, Lembas) rather than white's true top-end, while three board wipes sit awkwardly in a creature-forward deck. It plays like the average community build of the commander — reasonable, coherent, no sharp edge.
- 6. Modern tempo, tournament: graded good, judged baseline. The mana base and the interaction suite (Counterspell, Bolt, Consider) are legitimate, but the payoff half is a mess: 4 Temporal Mastery with only six cantrips to set up Miracle is close to a dead card, and 4 five-mana Enduring Curiosity sits atop a curve that wants to be tempo. With no Murktide, Ragavan, DRC, or any real clock, it draws cards and answers spells while never closing, which puts it at precon-adjacent power despite the clean lands.
- 7. Modern burn, casual: graded bad, judged bad. Modern burn wants Goblin Guide, Monastery Swiftspear, Lava Spike, Boros Charm and a Sacred Foundry-fetch mana base; instead this runs 24 Mountains and expensive filler like Akki Lavarunner, Fuming Effigy, Keral Keep Disciples and Browbeat that deal far too little damage for their cost. The 'punisher' theme (Risk Factor, Browbeat, Roiling Vortex) is inconsistent since opponents simply choose the mode that hurts least, and Eidolon plus Roiling Vortex also punish the pilot's own low-impact plan. The curve is bloated for an aggro deck with almost no reach beyond four Bolts, putting it below a precon in real power.
- 8. Modern lifegain, FNM: graded bad, judged bad. This is a lifegain kitchen-table pile in Modern: Arcane Signet isn't even legal/playable here as a Commander staple and is dead ramp, and payoffs like Bloodbond Vampire, Attended Healer, Bloodthirsty Conqueror and Enduring Tenacity are far too slow and clunky for the format. The mana base is 24 lands with only four duals for a deck full of WW/BB and four-drops, so colors and curve both stumble. Solitude and Sheoldred are strong cards stranded in a shell that can't support them, and interaction is minimal.
- 9. Standard midrange, FNM: graded bad, judged typical. But the plan is muddled: eight protection spells want cheap threats to defend, yet the only early bodies are two Llanowar Elves and four Darkstar Augur, and four Phyrexian Arena alongside Augur and Annex stacks redundant draw over a curve that clogs at five and six. It plays like a reasonable community midrange build rather than a tuned list."}
- 10. Standard aggro, tournament: graded bad, judged bad. The mana base is 24 basics with zero duals or Mountain/Plains-fixing lands in a deck full of double-pip costs like Boros Charm (RR) and Redcap Gutter-Dweller (BB/hybrid pressure), so colored requirements will routinely go unmet. The threats are a scattered mix of 2-ofs across unrelated tribes with no coherent aggro payoff, and 12 total lands of each type plus a top-heavy set of four-drops means the curve stumbles. It is below precon consistency despite a few individually strong cards like Warleader's Call and Boros Charm.
- 11. Commander with a locked card: graded bad, judged good. The aristocrats package is coherent and well-picked "" Yawgmoth, Grave Pact, Dictate of Erebos, Phyrexian/Ashnod's Altar, Pitiless Plunderer, Bastion of Remembrance and a deep stack of free sac outlets all point the same direction, and the drain effects genuinely feed Karlov's counters. Weak points keep it out of the top tier: essentially no fast mana or rocks beyond Sol Ring, thin targeted interaction (the answers are mostly board wipes), and a mana base that is 29 basics with only a handful of duals. As built it's a focused, on-plan deck that would grind out wins in a pod but lacks the speed and answers of a tuned list.
- 12. Commander on a budget: graded bad, judged baseline. The token theme is coherent — Adeline plus Divine Visitation, Hero of Bladehold, Intangible Virtue and mass go-wide payoffs all point the same way — but the card quality is precon-level. The ramp suite is a pile of marginal treasure trinkets (Coin of Mastery, Collector's Vault, Goldvein Pick, Charisma Bobblehead) with no Sol Ring, Smothering Tithe, or fast mana, and interaction skips Swords to Plowshares/Path/Enlightened Tutor for Kellan's Lightblades and Shire Shirriff. 38 lands of mostly untapped Plains is fine, but the curve is top-heavy and the deck will land its payoffs a turn or two after a tuned list already won.
- 13. owned first, and the commander is not owned: graded bad, judged typical. The removal suite is strong (Swords, Push, Get Lost, Bitter Triumph) and there's a solid lifegain-payoff core, but 36 basics with only a handful of rocks and no Command Tower/duals or Ghostly Prison/Vault of the Archangel-style Karlov staples makes the mana plain and the ramp thin. Many of the 'other' slots are unfocused mid-curve lifegain bodies rather than repeatable per-life triggers, so the deck gains life without converting it into a fast win. It's the honest average Karlov build: coherent plan, no sharp edge.
- 14. the user delegates the commander: graded bad, judged typical. This is a straightforward Gruul dragon tribal build \u2014 on-theme payoffs (Dragon Tempest, Scourge of Valkas, Lathliss, Terror of the Peaks-style effects) and Orb of Dragonkind ramp, with a decent but land-heavy mana base including Taiga and Cavern of Souls. But it leans on filler beaters and weak ramp (Dragonstorm Globe, Carnelian Orb, Kargan Dragonrider, Slumbering Dragon) rather than fast mana or strong interaction, and the curve is very top-heavy with only a handful of removal pieces. It's the deck the average dragon player builds: fun, coherent, no sharp edge.
- 15. delegated commander, owned first: graded bad, judged typical. This is a coherent W/B lifegain deck with good removal (Swords, Get Lost, Infernal Grasp, Bitter Triumph) and solid two-mana rocks, but the mana base is 24 basics plus mostly pain/utility lands with no real duals, and the payoff creatures (Angel of Vitality, Compassionate Healer, Shattered Angel) are low-impact fair cards rather than format staples. Card draw is serviceable but slow, and there's little fast mana or protection beyond Greaves/Boots. It plays like a well-upgraded average build of the archetype, not a tuned list.
- 16. a tight budget, owned first: graded bad, judged typical. This is a solid, on-theme WB aristocrats/sacrifice build with a good density of drain payoffs (Blood Artist, Zulaport, Bastion of Remembrance, Elas il-Kor) and clean removal, but it's short on token generators to actually feed the sac outlets and leans on mediocre filler like Explorer's Scope, Take Up the Shield, and Study Hall. The mana base is 38 lands that are almost all basics with no real duals or fast lands, and the ramp stops at generic two-mana rocks. Perfectly functional casual-table deck with no sharp edge or fast clock.
- 17. upgrade a precon, owned first: graded good, judged typical. This is a coherent Zada goblin-tribal build with the right skeleton \u2014o cantrips to copy, token makers, Impact Tremors/Goblin Bombardment sac outlets, and rituals \u2014 but it hedges between a Zada cantrip-combo deck and a generic goblin kindred pile, so neither plan is fully assembled. The mana base is 25 basics plus utility lands with almost no fast mana beyond Sol Ring and Ruby Medallion, and interaction is thin (Chaos Warp, Vandalblast) with no protection for a commander the whole deck depends on. Solid, on-theme, and about where the average community Zada list sits.
- 18. upgrade a precon, any card: graded good, judged typical. This is a five-color TMNT theme build leaning on the set's own cards plus a handful of genuine staples (Sol Ring, Esper Sentinel, Mystic Remora, Assassin's Trophy, Generous Gift), so the plan and interaction are coherent but unremarkable. The mana base is the weak point: five colors supported almost entirely by taplands, Thriving lands and basics with no real duals, fetches, or fast rocks, which will stumble on curve. Card quality is a step above precon but well short of tuned, with several low-impact set cards and no strong engine or win line.
- 19. the Hobbit family, two colours: graded bad, judged baseline. The mana base is 36 basics with zero duals, fetches, or fixing beyond Wayfarer's Bauble, which is untenable for a three-color Sultai deck full of double-pip LOTR commons. Card quality is largely limited-level Mirkwood/spider filler with only a handful of real staples (The One Ring, Orcish Bowmasters, Delighted Halfling), and the synergy is diffuse rather than pointed at a Thranduil payoff. It plays about like an off-the-shelf precon: functional, slow, and generic.
- 20. the Hobbit family, a delegated commander: graded bad, judged baseline. This is essentially a mono-red set-themed pile: 31 basic Mountains with almost no fixing or utility land beyond a couple of flavor picks, one Signet plus a few slow ramp rocks like Wayfarer's Bauble, and a creature suite of generic mid-curve bodies (Oliphaunt, Olog-hai Crusher, Troop of Ponies) that don't build toward any single plan. A handful of genuinely strong cards — The One Ring, Mox Amber, Inferno Titan, Goblin Cratermaker — sit next to an unfocused mix of Equipment payoffs with too few carriers and situational removal. It plays and does red things, but it's precon-grade in speed, card quality, and cohesion.
- 21. the Hobbit family, mana from outside: graded bad, judged typical. This is a heavily on-theme LOTR/Smaug build with a real mana base — fetchlands, Blood Crypt, Ancient Tomb, Command Tower — and the format's staple engines in The One Ring, Orcish Bowmasters and Mox Amber. But the bulk of the deck is set-flavored filler (Well-Worn Spatula, Oliphaunt, Desert Were-Worm, Long-Lost Lances) and a scattered equipment/goblin/dragon plan that never commits to one axis, with weak ramp and thin targeted interaction. It's a well-supported casual list around its commander rather than a tuned deck.
- 22. a set family and a card from outside it: graded bad, judged bad. The mana base is 36 unadorned basics split evenly across three colors with almost no fixing, so double-costed and off-color spells like Languish, Mox Amber, and the Sultai gold cards will strand regularly. Card quality is largely draft filler (Gigantic Big Bear, Nasty Little Rabbit, Mirkwood Elk, Colossal Whale) with no coherent elf or ward/keyword payoff plan behind Thranduil, and only a handful of real cards (Sol Ring, The One Ring, Orcish Bowmasters, Delighted Halfling) carry it. That combination of broken color requirements and a top-heavy pile of unsynergistic bodies sits below a preconstructed deck.
- 23. two set families at once: graded bad, judged typical. A mono-white token/kindred build with a coherent plan (Andúril, Jazal, Maskwood Nexus, go-wide payoffs and draw engines like Skullclamp, Caretaker's Talent, Mentor of the Meek) and solid staple removal in Swords, Skyclave, Generous Gift. But the mana is 23 basics plus utility lands with little fixing needed, ramp is mostly two- and three-mana rocks, and there's a fair amount of filler (Iron Hills Blacksmith, Bumbleflower's Sharepot, Psychosis Crawler, Sunscorch Regent) alongside four board wipes that fight its own token board. That's the community-average version of the deck: reasonable, on-theme, no sharp edge.
- 24. a 60-card deck from one set: graded bad, judged baseline. The prowess-ish shell has a few real Modern cards (Emberheart Challenger, Artist's Talent, Abrade) but the payoffs are undercut by a spell suite of Sazacap's Brew and Agate Assault rather than Lightning Bolt, Ral's Reinforcements/Monastery Swiftspear-tier one-drops, or any burn reach. Arcane Signet is not a legal-plan card here and 24 basics with zero utility lands or Mishra's Bauble-style cantrips signals a kitchen-table build. It curves out and casts its spells, so it's precon-level playable but far off a league-winning record.

Verdict: FAIL.
