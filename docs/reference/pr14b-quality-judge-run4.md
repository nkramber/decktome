## The judge's reasons

- 1. lifegain Commander, any card: graded bad, judged typical. This is the standard community lifegain build for Karlov \u2014 all the expected payoffs (Heliod, Archangel of Thune, Vito, Well of Lost Dreams, Ocelot Pride) with coherent synergy, but the mana base is 24 basics plus a dozen taplands/painlands with no fetches or fast lands, and ramp leans on slow rocks like Orazca Relic and The Celestus. Interaction is thin and mostly reactive protection, and there's little tutoring or a compact win line beyond durdling into a big lifetotal. Solid and on-plan, just no sharp edge.
- 2. aristocrats Commander, owned first: graded typical, judged good. Solid Orzhov midrange build around Denethor's Ring-bearer/sacrifice and -1/-1 counter theme, with strong card quality: Swords, Fatal Push, Get Lost, Infernal Grasp, Orcish Bowmasters, Skullclamp, Sol Ring and clean commander protection. The mana base is functional but budget-basic-heavy with no real duals or fetches, and the ramp package is mostly two-mana rocks, so it's a step slower than top lists. Coherent, well-interactive, and above the community average without the sharp efficiency of a tuned build.
- 3. artifacts Commander, bracket 4: graded bad, judged typical. It's a coherent mono-blue artifact deck with a few real cards (Urza's Saga, Mox Opal, Metalworker, Mystic Forge, Emry, Transmute Artifact), but the bulk is durdly filler like Chrome Steed, Panther Robot, Jhoira's Familiar and weak removal such as Water Whip and Lumengrid Drake. It lacks the fast mana, tutors, and combo lines (Winter Orb, Isochron/Dramatic, Thopter-Sword, free counters) that make Urza a cEDH deck, and 22 basic Islands with almost no ritual acceleration keeps it slow. Reasonable and on-theme, but no edge.
- 4. dinosaur tribal, bracket 2: graded bad, judged typical. This is the standard community build of Gishath: real dual/tribal lands, a solid ramp package (Sol Ring, Nature's Lore, Farseek, mana dorks) and a couple of genuine staples like Sylvan Library and Skullclamp, all pointed at cheating dinos into play. But the creature suite is padded with filler beaters (Nest Robber, Dromosaur, Soaring Sandwing, Pterodon Knight, Harnessed Snubhorn) that do nothing for the plan beyond being a body, and interaction is thin \u2014 mostly protection plus a few fight effects and board wipes that can catch its own team. Coherent and reasonably curved, but no sharp edge or top-end efficiency.
- 5. blink Commander, owned first: graded typical, judged typical. It's a coherent mono-white Gilraen blink/ETB deck with the expected staples (Sol Ring, Swords to Plowshares, Skullclamp, Giada, Palace Jailer, Angel of Serenity) and a healthy count of removal and protection, but nothing that pushes past the average community build. The mana base wastes slots on fixing a mono-color deck doesn't need — City of Brass, Spire of Industry, Exotic Orchard, Chromatic Lantern, Fellwar Stone — and the tri-lands/fetch-basics only slow it down. Card quality is reasonable and the blink synergies line up, but the curve is top-heavy and there's little in the way of fast mana or proactive threat, so it lands squarely at the format's median.
- 6. Modern tempo, tournament: graded typical, judged typical. The shell is real Modern Izzet tempo "Ragavan, Ledger Shredder, Bolt, Force of Negation, cantrips" on a clean two-color mana base, so it is well above precon level. But four Temporal Mastery is a liability: with only six cantrips there is not enough top-deck manipulation to miracle it reliably, and hard-casting a seven-drop in a tempo deck is a dead card. Missing the format's actual payoffs (Murktide, Bowmasters, Bauble/Expressive Iteration density) leaves it as a reasonable but unsharpened version of the archetype.
- 7. Modern burn, casual: graded bad, judged bad. Twenty-four basic Mountains with no fetch/Ramp package and no utility lands is a wasteful mana base for a deck whose curve is jammed with four-drops (Ashcloud Phoenix, Hazoret, Torbran, Sunspine Lynx) that Modern burn never plays. Guttersnipe and Ancestral Anger are draft-tier filler that actively fight the plan, and Hazoret is nearly uncastable-active alongside a grindy creature suite. Only Bolt and Lava Dart are format-caliber, so this sits below even a precon's coherence.
- 8. Modern lifegain, FNM: graded bad, judged bad. The mana base is actually fine and Solitude/Deep-Cavern Bat are real Modern cards, but the rest is a kitchen-table lifegain pile: Bloodbond Vampire, Twinblade Paladin, Ajani's Pridemate and Enduring Tenacity are far too slow and fragile for the format, and there's no way to convert incidental lifegain into a win before turn six or seven. The Arcane Signet/Fellwar Stone package is a Commander import that does nothing here as a pair of singleton two-drops in a two-color deck, and Lembas as a four-of is a wasted slot. With interaction limited to four Solitudes off almost no white creatures to pitch, this plays below a precon.
- 9. Standard midrange, FNM: graded bad, judged baseline. The individual cards are fine Standard-quality staples (Bitter Triumph, Vein Ripper, Rottenmouth Viper) and the two-color mana base with Overgrown Tomb and Blooming Marsh is clean, but the curve is badly top-heavy: twelve six-drops supported by only 24 lands and two Llanowar Elves means the deck routinely stalls out. The protection suite of Snakeskin Veil and Undying Malice has almost nothing cheap to protect, so it sits dead early, and Maelstrom Pulse is a legality question mark. It plays like a precon: strong headline creatures, no engine to reliably cast them.
- 10. Standard aggro, tournament: graded baseline, judged good. This is a coherent Boros aggro/midrange shell with real Standard staples \u2014 Slickshot Show-Off, Boros Charm, Sacred Foundry/Inspiring Vantage/Sunbillow Verge \u2014 and a clean two-color mana base at 24 lands with a low curve. Removal is a bit off-rate (Harsh Annotation and Krenko's Buzzcrusher over cleaner answers) and the mustering/valiant payoffs pull in slightly different directions, so it lacks the focus of a top-8 list. Solid enough to post a winning record in a league without being tuned to the format's best.
- 11. Commander with a locked card: graded bad, judged typical. It's a coherent Orzhov aristocrats build with a strong core of sac outlets, payoffs and free-sac fodder engines (Yawgmoth, Priest of Forgotten Gods, Pitiless Plunderer, Meathook, Dictate), but the mana base is 36 basics with only Sol Ring for ramp, which is well below format standard. Token production is thin for a deck built on sacrificing bodies, and there's essentially no counterplay or targeted interaction beyond creature removal, plus filler like Sunstone, Promise of Tomorrow and Demon of Catastrophes. Also notable that the deck barely leans on the commander's lifegain-counter plan, so it reads as an average community aristocrats list rather than a tuned one.
- 12. Commander on a budget: graded bad, judged typical. The token/anthem plan is coherent and on-theme with real payoffs (Hero of Bladehold, Wedding Announcement, Divine Visitation, Retrofitter Foundry, Skyclave Apparition), and 38 lands support the curve fine. But the ramp suite is a pile of low-impact treasure trinkets (Prying Blade, Noble's Purse, Coin of Mastery, Charisma Bobblehead) in place of Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Smothering Tithe, and Land Tax, and interaction leans on clunky removal instead of Swords to Plowshares/Path/Mana Tithe. That puts it right at the community-average build for Adeline: functional and synergistic, no sharp edge.
- 13. owned first, and the commander is not owned: graded bad, judged typical. The removal package is genuinely good for a casual list (Swords, Fatal Push, Get Lost, Infernal Grasp, Generous Gift) and the two-color all-basics mana works fine, but the deck is a pile of small lifegain creatures with few real payoffs \u2014 no Vito, Sanguine Bond, Serra Ascendant, or Cradle of Vitality to convert life totals into wins, and Karlov's counters are underleveraged. Three board wipes in a go-wide creature deck actively fight the plan, and the lack of any nonbasic utility or fixing lands caps the ceiling. That's the average community Karlov build: coherent theme, reasonable interaction, no sharp edge.

That puts it right at the community average build rather than anything sharp.
