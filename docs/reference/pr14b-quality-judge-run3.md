## The judge's reasons

- 1. lifegain Commander, any card: graded bad, judged typical. The lifegain-counters core is coherent and has some genuinely strong pieces (Heliod, Vito, Well of Lost Dreams, Ocelot Pride, Serra Ascendant, Solitude), which is why it clears precon level. But the ramp package is near-unplayable filler \u2014 Colossal Plow, Hot Dog Cart, Bounty Board, Nuka-Cola Vending Machine \u2014 with no Sol Ring, Signet, or Talisman, and the mana base is 27 basics with only a handful of duals. Interaction is thin and mostly conditional protection rather than answers, so it plays like the average community Karlov build: on-plan but slow and unfocused.
- 2. aristocrats Commander, owned first: graded bad, judged typical. This is a solid, unfocused midrange Denethor build: good generic staples (Sol Ring, Skullclamp, Orcish Bowmasters, plenty of spot removal and three wraths) but the mana base is 27 basics with only a handful of duals and zero real fixing lands or fast mana beyond Sol Ring. The synergy is scattered \u2014 some sacrifice/aristocrat pieces, some equipment/Puresteel, some -1/-1 counter cards, none of them a coherent engine \u2014 and the commander protection suite outnumbers actual win conditions. It plays like a well-upgraded precon rather than a tuned list, which is the community average.
- 3. artifacts Commander, bracket 4: graded bad, judged baseline. It's a coherent blue artifact pile with Urza's synergy pieces (Chief Engineer, Etherium Sculptor, Emry, Metalworker, Mystic Forge), but the card quality is precon-grade: Cargo Ship, Rust Elemental, Panther Robot, Solar Array, Coin of Mastery and creature-only removal like Water Whip and Unable to Scream are far off the pace. Fast mana is essentially just Mox Opal with no Sol Ring/Crypt/Vault, and 20 Islands over the format's fixing and card selection means the engine turns on late. There is also no win condition or combo package — it grinds value rather than closing, and the interaction is soft mana-cost counters instead of free spells.
- 4. dinosaur tribal, bracket 2: graded bad, judged typical. The mana base is genuinely strong for a three-color tribal deck and the payoff shell (Gishath plus Pantlaza, Regisaur Alpha, Garruk's Uprising, Marauding Raptor) is on plan, but the creature suite is padded with weak filler like Raptor Companion, Colossal Dreadmaw, Dinosaur Egg, Nest Robber and Drowsing Tyrannodon. Ramp is thin for a curve this top-heavy, interaction is mostly narrow fight/bite effects, and three symmetrical wipes fight your own board. It reads as the standard community Gishath build: functional and synergistic, without the tuning or efficiency of a winning list.
- 5. blink Commander, owned first: graded typical, judged typical. It's a coherent mono-white Gilraen blink/protection build with the expected staples (Sol Ring, Swords to Plowshares, Skullclamp, Swiftfoot Boots, Angel of Sanctions/Serenity) and a mana base that works fine because it's essentially mono-colored. But the card quality thins out fast into filler and off-plan picks (PuPu UFO, Mirror of Galadriel, Stone of Erech, Westfold Rider, Bender's Waterskin), and running three sweepers alongside a creature/aura-heavy plan pulls against itself. Ramp is all rocks with no fast mana or strong lands, so it plays as a solid but blunt average build of this commander.
- 6. Modern tempo, tournament: graded typical, judged typical. The mana base and interaction suite (Counterspell, Bolt, Consider, Ledger Shredder) are legitimately Modern-caliber, but the deck is 24 lands with a very thin, clunky top end and no real clock beyond two-power fliers. Temporal Mastery at four copies with no miracle enablers or fetches is a liability, and Enduring Curiosity/Faerie Mastermind are value pieces rather than the pressure a tempo deck needs, so it plays like a competent but unfocused draw-go pile.
- 7. Modern burn, casual: graded bad, judged bad. It's a mono-red burn shell without the burn: no Lava Spike, Skewer, Rift Bolt, Boros Charm, or Swiftspear/Goblin Guide, and instead four-drops like Akki Lavarunner, Fuming Effigy, and Keral Keep Disciples that are draft filler and don't advance a reach plan. 24 Mountains is several lands too many for a deck whose curve should top at two, and symmetrical damage from Eidolon/Roiling Vortex plus Risk Factor/Browbeat giving the opponent the choice makes the clock unreliable. Even a precon offers more coherent synergy than this.
- 8. Modern lifegain, FNM: graded bad, judged bad. The mana base is 24 lands with only 4 duals plus Arcane Signet, a Commander-only card that's not even legal in Modern, and the deck is full of Standard-level lifegain payoffs (Ajani's Pridemate, Bloodbond Vampire, Attended Healer) that die to every removal spell and do nothing to affect the board. Curve is clogged at four and five with almost no interaction beyond Solitude and two Riders, so it's far too slow for a format defined by turn-three kills and free spells. Solitude and Sheoldred are strong cards stranded in a shell that can't support them.
- 9. Standard midrange, FNM: graded bad, judged typical. The mana base is clean and the golgari midrange shell has real payoffs in Vein Ripper and Vaultborn Tyrant, but the deck is pulled in two directions: four Undying Malice and four Snakeskin Veil are protection spells for a board it rarely has early, and Phyrexian Arena as a four-of is a slow, non-interactive draw engine the top lists have moved past. Curve is top-heavy with six six-drops plus Goldvein Hydra and only two Llanowar Elves to bridge, so the fast starts of the format punish it. Removal count is respectable, which keeps it reasonable rather than weak.
- 10. Standard aggro, tournament: graded bad, judged typical. The Boros aggro shell has a real plan — cheap threats plus Warleader's Call and Case of the Gateway Express — and enough removal, but the mana base is 24 basics with zero duals or Battlefield Forge/Sunbaked Canyon-style fixing, which strains the double-pip costs on Boros Charm and Warleader's Call. Several slots (Bedhead Beastie, Irreverent Gremlin, Frilled Sparkshooter, two-ofs of Fugitive Codebreaker and Reckless Lackey) are filler rather than the format's best one-drops, and the split two-ofs blunt consistency. It plays like a competent community build of the archetype without the tuning of a Challenge list.

- 12. Commander on a budget: graded bad, judged baseline. The token theme is coherent and there are real payoffs (Divine Visitation, Hero of Bladehold, Defiler of Faith, Wedding Announcement), but the deck skips nearly every white staple \u2014 no Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Smothering Tithe, Land Tax, Anointed Procession, Cathars' Crusade, Swords to Plowshares or Path to Exile. Instead the ramp slot is filled with slow Treasure trinkets (Coin of Mastery, Goldvein Pick, Collector's Vault, Bucknard's Everfull Purse) that cost mana without accelerating meaningfully, and interaction is a handful of overpriced removal plus three symmetrical wipes that fight its own board. Mana is a fine 38 lands of mostly Plains, so it functions, but at roughly precon speed and power.
- 13. owned first, and the commander is not owned: graded bad, judged typical. The core is fine — Sol Ring, Skullclamp, premium single-target removal, and a real lifegain-trigger shell for Karlov — but the mana base is 36 basics with no Godless Shrine, Fetid Heath, Command Tower, or fetches, and ramp stops at two mana rocks plus baubles. Too many payoffs are weak vanilla lifegain bodies (Angel of Vitality, Shattered Angel, Kor Firewalker, Compassionate Healer) rather than the format's engines like Ajani's Pridemate stacks, Bolas's Citadel, or fast Voltron protection, and there's almost no free interaction. It plays like the community's average Karlov build: coherent plan, dull curve, no sharp edge.
- 14. the user delegates the commander: graded bad, judged good. The dragon payoffs (Dragon Tempest, Scourge of Valkas, Lathliss, Utvara, Thrakkus) line up coherently for a fast damage-based kill. It's above the average tribal pile, but the top end is heavy, targeted interaction is thin, and cards like Tibalt's Trickery and Slumbering Dragon keep it out of the top bracket.

It's clearly above precon in card quality and interaction, yet lacks the tutors, cheap combo lines, or protection density that a tuned list would run.

- 17. upgrade a precon, owned first: graded typical, judged typical. This is a reasonable Zada/goblins hybrid with the right skeleton \u2014 Skullclamp, Sol Ring, Goblin Bombardment, Impact Tremors, token makers and a handful of cantrips to copy \u2014 but it hedges between the Zada spell-copy plan and a generic goblin tribal build, so neither line is fully supported (only a handful of one-target cantrips, and many goblins that do nothing with the commander). - 18. upgrade a precon, any card: graded typical, judged typical. This is a five-color commander built mostly out of on-theme TMNT set cards plus a light sprinkle of format staples (Sol Ring, Esper Sentinel, Mystic Remora, Assassin's Trophy, Generous Gift), which reads as a lightly upgraded precon-style build. The mana base is the real problem: for WUBRG it leans on taplands, Thriving cycle, and basics with almost no real duals or fetch lands, so the curve of five-color spells will stumble. Synergy with the mutant/counter theme is coherent and there's decent removal and card draw, but nothing sharp enough to beat a well-tuned table.
- 19. the Hobbit family, two colours: graded bad, judged baseline. The mana base is 36 basic lands with no duals, fetches, or fixing beyond Wayfarer's Bauble for a three-color deck, which will badly stumble on casting Thranduil and the many multicolor spells. Card quality is heavily LOTR-set-limited filler (Mirkwood Elk, Elven Raft-Steerer, Nimrodel Watcher) with only a few real bombs like The One Ring and Orcish Bowmasters, and the elf-ramp package is thin. It's a coherent Elves/Mirkwood theme but plays and functions at roughly precon speed.
- 20. the Hobbit family, a delegated commander: graded bad, judged baseline. This is essentially a mono-red LOTR-themed pile: 31 basic Mountains, almost no real ramp beyond Arcane Signet/Wayfarer's Bauble, and a top-heavy mass of on-theme creatures like Oliphaunt, Olog-hai Crusher and Desert Were-Worm that do little for a coherent plan. It gestures at both Equipment/Voltron and big-creature beatdown without committing to either, and interaction is limited to small burn rather than the format's efficient answers. The One Ring, Mox Amber and Palantír are genuine power, but the surrounding cards and clunky curve keep it at precon level.
- 21. the Hobbit family, mana from outside: graded bad, judged typical. It's a coherent LOTR-flavored treasure/equipment Rakdos build with real staples (The One Ring, Orcish Bowmasters, Ancient Tomb, a full fetch suite) but the bulk of the list is set-limited filler like Well-Worn Spatula, Oliphaunt, and Desert Were-Worm that underperforms in Commander. Interaction is thin and slow, the curve is top-heavy for a deck with only ~10 rocks/ramp pieces, and the fetches barely matter with two shocks/duals to find. Solid casual synergy, no sharp edge.
- 22. a set family and a card from outside it: graded bad, judged bad. Three colors supported by nothing but 36 untapped basics with zero duals, fetches, or fixing beyond Wood Elves/Thr\u00f3r's Map \u2014 a mana base worse than any precon, which will strand the many double-costed and top-end cards. The card pool is largely filler Hobbit/Middle-earth commons and one-off beasts with no coherent elf or ETB payoff engine, so the good pieces (The One Ring, Sol Ring, Orcish Bowmasters, Delighted Halfling) sit in a random pile rather than a plan. Curve is top-heavy and interaction is slow, sorcery-speed removal.
- 23. two set families at once: graded typical, judged typical. This is a solid mono-white go-wide/typal build with the expected staples (Sol Ring, Swords, Skyclave Apparition, Skullclamp, Caretaker's Talent) and a clean mana base, but it hedges between hobbit/food, artifact, and generic token payoffs without a tight engine. Card quality is fine yet unremarkable, ramp is mostly two-mana rocks, and the wipes sit awkwardly in a deck that wants a wide board. It plays like a well-upgraded average build rather than a sharp one.
- 24. a 60-card deck from one set: graded bad, judged baseline. Mono-red prowess pieces are here but the deck has almost no cheap spells to trigger them \u2014 Artist's Talent and removal alone won't turn on Emberheart Challenger, and there are no burn spells, no Ragavan/Dragon's Rage Channeler/Lightning Bolt-tier cards, and no fetch/Sokenzan-style manabase. Arcane Signet isn't even legal in Modern, and 24 lands with a top-heavy creature curve makes it clunky. It plays like a limited-quality aggro pile: functional, on-color, but far off any competitive Modern list.

Verdict: FAIL.
