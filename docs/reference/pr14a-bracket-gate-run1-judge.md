## Why

### 1. Bracket 1, Gishath, Sun's Avatar, dinosaurs

Judge: bracket 2. This is a straightforward dinosaur tribal battlecruiser list with no Game Changers, no infinite combos, no mass land denial, and no extra turns. Ramp is basic (signet, land-fetch sorceries, mana dorks) and interaction is limited to a few board wipes and combat tricks, so it wins slowly by swinging with big creatures. That puts it right around precon-plus power, comfortably in Core.

### 2. Bracket 1, Karlov of the Ghost Council, lifegain

Judge: bracket 3. This is an upgraded lifegain/aristocrats build with one Game Changer (Solitude), a decent but unhurried mana base with no real fast mana, and several two-card infinite combos \u2014 Heliod, Sun-Crowned + Archangel of Thune, and Vito/Defiant Bloodlord + Cliffhaven Vampire. Those combos cost eight or more mana total, so they aren't cheap early-game kills, and the deck's clock is a grindy Karlov value plan rather than a turn-four win. That places it comfortably in Upgraded rather than Core or Optimized.

### 3. Bracket 1, Adeline, Resplendent Cathar, tokens

Judge: bracket 3. This is a focused mono-white go-wide token deck whose payoff density (Mondrak, Ojer Taq, Divine Visitation, Hero of Bladehold, Archangel Elspeth, Halo Fountain) and card-advantage engines push it clearly past precon power, even though it runs no Game Changers, no fast mana, no tutors, and a slow 25-Plains mana base. Its clock depends on stacking anthems and doublers over several turns, with removal limited to a handful of flexible white answers, so it lands in the mid-speed six-turn-plus range rather than optimized territory. No mass land denial, no chained extra turns, and no cheap two-card infinite combo keeps it comfortably legal for Upgraded.

### 4. Bracket 2, Gishath, Sun's Avatar, dinosaurs

Judge: bracket 2. This is a straightforward tribal Dinosaur deck built around Gishath's combat trigger, with no Game Changers, no infinite combos, no extra turns, and no mass land denial. Its ramp is basic (Signet, Cultivate, Rampant Growth, mana dorks), interaction is limited to a few wraths and Savage Stomp, and it needs to connect in combat to snowball, so games run long. That's precon-plus consistency at best, squarely Core rather than Upgraded.

### 5. Bracket 2, Karlov of the Ghost Council, lifegain

Judge: bracket 3. This is a lifegain/aristocrats build with a slow, basic-heavy mana base and no fast mana or tutors, but it does exceed precon power with premium interaction (Solitude, Ajani Strength of the Pride, wraths) and the Game Changer Vein Ripper. It packs the Heliod, Sun-Crowned + Archangel of Thune infinite combo plus Aetherflux Reservoir as an alternate kill, though at ten combined mana that combo won't reliably assemble in the first six turns. That profile
tolerated combo, one Game Changer, upgraded-but-grindy pace lands it squarely in Upgraded.

### 6. Bracket 2, Adeline, Resplendent Cathar, tokens

Judge: bracket 2. This is a mono-white token deck with 37 basic Plains, no ramp beyond Keeper of the Accord/Oketra's Monument, no tutors, no fast mana, and no Game Changers or two-card infinite combos. Interaction is limited to a handful of removal spells and a couple of wraths, and the wincon is grinding out tokens over many turns. That puts it right around precon power, slightly better in synergy but far too slow and unfocused for Bracket 3.

### 7. Bracket 3, Karlov of the Ghost Council, lifegain

Judge: bracket 3. It's a lifegain/Karlov value deck with a fair mana base and no fast mana, but it does pack a couple of Game Changers (Tymna the Weaver, plus premium cards like Solitude and Vein Ripper) and an infinite combo in Heliod, Sun-Crowned + Archangel of Thune (backed by Vito/Defiant Bloodlord win conditions and Aetherflux Reservoir). Since that combo costs eight mana total and the deck grinds through incremental lifegain triggers, it's not landing reliably in the first six turns. That places it as an upgraded, above-precon build rather than an optimized one.

### 8. Bracket 3, Denethor, Ruling Steward, aristocrats sacrifice

Judge: bracket 3. This is a tuned Orzhov aristocrats deck with two Game Changers (Yawgmoth, Thran Physician and Grave Pact), a solid but not fast mana base (a couple of shocks/duals, Phyrexian Tower, no Sol Ring-tier fast mana or heavy tutoring), and lots of redundant sac outlets and drain payoffs. It has combo potential (free sac outlets plus Phyrexian/Ashnod's Altar, Blasting Station, Yawgmoth loops), but the pieces are mostly three-plus mana and assemble around turn six or later rather than as a cheap early two-card kill. That mix of upgraded power, a few Game Changers, and no mass land denial or chained extra turns lands squarely in Upgraded.

### 9. Bracket 3, Zada, Hedron Grinder, goblins

Judge: bracket 3. Two Game Changers (The One Ring, Jeska's Will) plus Sol Ring/rituals and a solid tribal engine put this well beyond precon power, but the mana base is largely basics and utility lands with no heavy tutor suite or fast mana beyond Sol Ring. The Conspicuous Snoop + Kiki-Jiki line is an infinite, but at four and five mana with no tutors to assemble it, it's unlikely inside the first six turns. That's textbook Upgraded: fast, resilient goblin aggro that usually closes around turn six to eight.

### 10. Bracket 4, Urza, Lord High Artificer, artifacts

Judge: bracket 4. This is a tuned Urza artifact-combo deck: Mishra's Workshop, Mox Opal, Gemstone Caverns, Metalworker, Grand Architect and cost reducers can produce explosive turn 2-4 mana, and Urza himself plus KCI, Mystic Forge, Emry, Escape Protocol/Ghostly Flicker give easy engine loops and near-infinite mana lines. Between the Game Changers (Urza, Mishra's Workshop, Gemstone Caverns) and the fast, redundant combo/counterspell shell, it plays well past the Upgraded ceiling. It stops short of cEDH only because of jankier filler (Chrome Steed, Water Whip, Jhoira's Familiar) and a lack of the best blue tutors and rituals, so it lands in Optimized.

### 11. Bracket 4, Korvold, Fae-Cursed King, treasure sacrifice

Judge: bracket 3. This is a well-tuned Korvold aristocrats/sacrifice build with two Game Changers (Yawgmoth, Thran Physician and Grist, the Hunger Tide) plus quality tutors like Razaketh, Sidisi, and Diabolic Intent, so it sits above precon power. However, the mana base has no true fast mana (only Phyrexian Tower and treasure makers), the interaction is mostly incidental sacrifice-based removal, and the combo lines (Blasting Station/Goblin Bombardment loops, Yawgmoth engines) need multiple pieces and aren't landing cheaply by turn six. That places it squarely in Upgraded rather than Optimized.

### 12. Bracket 4, Prosper, Tome-Bound, treasure

Judge: bracket 4. The deck runs four Game Changers \u2014 Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer, The One Ring, Wheel of Fortune, and Ancient Copper Dragon \u2014 which is one more than Bracket 3 permits, so it lands in Optimized by rule. Its play pattern supports that: a strong dual/triple-land mana base, Ragavan and treasure engines for fast mana, a deep card-draw suite (Black Market Connections, Skullclamp, Night's Whisper) and heavy interaction (Snuff Out, Deadly Rollick, Pyroblast, Terminate) backing a Prosper/Torment of Hailfire drain finish. There's no dedicated two-card infinite, so it's not a cEDH list, just a high-power Bracket 4 build.

### 13. Bracket 5, Kinnan, Bonder Prodigy, combo

Judge: bracket 4. This is a dedicated Kinnan untapper-combo list with fast mana (Grim Monolith, Mana Vault, Basalt Monolith, Gemstone Caverns), tutors (Worldly Tutor, Fabricate, Muddle the Mixture), and multiple cheap two-card infinites (Basalt Monolith + Voltaic Construct/Filigree Sages/Kelpie Guide, Devoted Druid + Pemmin's Aura, Staff of Domination) into Thassa's Oracle, so it can win by turn four. It also packs Game Changers like Cyclonic Rift and Thassa's Oracle plus free/cheap counterspells to protect the line, well past Bracket 3's limits. It falls short of cEDH only because the untap 'theme' pulls in fringe filler (Sokka's Haiku, Octopus Form, Twitch) instead of the format's best staples.

### 14. Bracket 5, Yuriko, the Tiger's Shadow, ninjas

Judge: bracket 5. This is a tuned cEDH-style Yuriko list: Underground Sea, Mox Diamond/Opal/Amber, Chrome Mox, Lotus Petal, Mana Vault, Dark Ritual and Gemstone Caverns for explosive starts, plus cantrip suites and a dense free-interaction package (Force of Will, Force of Negation, Fierce Guardianship, Pact of Negation, Flusterstorm, Mental Misstep, Deadly Rollick). It stacks numerous Game Changers (Rhystic Study, Mystic Remora, Underground Sea, Fierce Guardianship, Force of Negation, Mana Vault, Grim Monolith-tier fast mana) and aims to kill via commander damage as early as turns 3-4. That speed, free counterspell density and optimized mana base put it squarely at competitive tables rather than Bracket 4 pods.

### 15. Bracket 5, Najeela, the Blade-Blossom, warriors combat

Judge: bracket 3. This is a five-color Najeela warrior-tribal battlecruiser build: the mana base is just shocks and a few utility lands with no fast mana (no Sol Ring, Mana Crypt, or Jeweled Lotus) and essentially no tutors, and it's packed with midrange creatures rather than efficient interaction. Najeela plus Combat Celebrant/Lightning Runner does give repeatable extra-combat loops that can go infinite with a mana outlet, and it carries Game Changers like Winota, Joiner of Forces, so it's above precon power. The slow mana and clunky payoffs keep it from reliably ending games by turn four, so Upgraded fits best.
